# Third-party brand marks are never a deliverable

We considered opening a downloads section for AI vendor logos, and decided against
it: brand marks may be rendered only to *identify* a product we name or link, never
packaged, hosted or offered for download.

The path data we bundle comes from Simple Icons under CC0, but CC0 §4(a) states
that "no trademark or patent rights held by Affirmer are waived", and the Simple
Icons disclaimer is explicit that "Simple Icons is released under CC0 — though that
doesn't mean to imply that all icons within the project are also CC0." We checked
all 15 AI-vendor entries we bundle (anthropic, openai, google, deepseek, meta,
mistralai, qwen, ollama, huggingface, perplexity, cursor, v0, vercel, notion,
replit): **every one has an empty `license` field.** Simple Icons also maintains a
`removals@simpleicons.org` channel for brands that want out.

Rendering a mark beside the name of the thing it identifies is nominative use.
Serving the same mark as a download makes the trademark itself the product — a
different act, and the one that draws letters. The upside would not have repaid it
either: Simple Icons already does this at a scale we cannot match, and we link to
it from `/resources`, so we would have been competing with something we recommend.

## Consequences

Anything in the icon set must be first-party artwork for a *concept*, never a
company's mark. If a reader wants vendor logos, `/resources` points them at Simple
Icons — that is the right answer and we should keep giving it.
